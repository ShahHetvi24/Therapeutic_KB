# Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) — Biomarker / Risk Marker Summary


## 8. Biomarker / Risk Marker Summary

| Biomarker / Risk Marker | Clinical Role | Treatment / Market Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| del(17p) | High-risk cytogenetic abnormality | Avoid chemoimmunotherapy; targeted therapy favored |
| TP53 mutation | High-risk molecular marker | Strong driver of therapy selection; continuous BTK inhibitor often favored |
| IGHV unmutated | Higher-risk disease biology | Predicts shorter remissions with chemoimmunotherapy; supports targeted therapy selection |
| IGHV mutated | More favorable prognosis | Historically identified patients with durable FCR responses, but targeted therapy now dominant |
| del(11q) | Previously adverse-risk cytogenetic marker | Less predictive in targeted therapy era but still used in risk assessment |
| trisomy 12 | Intermediate-risk biological marker | Useful for characterization and prognosis |
| del(13q) isolated | More favorable-risk marker | Often associated with indolent disease |
| Complex karyotype | Adverse-risk marker | Higher relapse risk and treatment resistance |
| BTK mutation / PLCG2 mutation | Resistance mechanism after covalent BTKi | Supports switch to noncovalent BTKi or alternative class when clinically available |
| BCL2 mutation / clonal evolution | Resistance mechanism after venetoclax | Sequencing and trial relevance |
| MRD status | Depth-of-response marker | Increasingly important in fixed-duration therapy and trials |

NCCN Guidelines Insights 2026 highlight the importance of BTK inhibitor and BCL-2 inhibitor-containing regimens and the role of newer options such as pirtobrutinib and lisocabtagene maraleucel after prior targeted therapy exposure. [JNCCN CLL/SLL Insights 2026]
