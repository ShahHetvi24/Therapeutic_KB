# Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) — Classification and Staging


## 4. Classification and Staging

### 4.1 CLL vs SLL

| Entity | Primary Disease Location | Practical Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| CLL | Peripheral blood and bone marrow prominent | Typically identified by lymphocytosis and flow cytometry; managed using CLL/SLL treatment principles |
| SLL | Lymph node or tissue predominant, with fewer circulating cells | Biologically similar to CLL; often coded and tracked as lymphoma or CLL/SLL depending source |

### 4.2 Rai Staging

| Rai Stage | Typical Features | Risk Group |
|---|---|---|
| Rai 0 | Lymphocytosis only | Low risk |
| Rai I | Lymphocytosis plus lymphadenopathy | Intermediate risk |
| Rai II | Lymphocytosis plus splenomegaly and/or hepatomegaly, with or without lymphadenopathy | Intermediate risk |
| Rai III | Lymphocytosis plus anemia | High risk |
| Rai IV | Lymphocytosis plus thrombocytopenia | High risk |

### 4.3 Binet Staging

| Binet Stage | Typical Features | Risk Group |
|---|---|---|
| Binet A | Fewer than 3 involved lymphoid areas, no anemia or thrombocytopenia | Lower risk |
| Binet B | 3 or more involved lymphoid areas, no anemia or thrombocytopenia | Intermediate risk |
| Binet C | Anemia and/or thrombocytopenia regardless of lymphoid areas | Higher risk |

### 4.4 Treatment-Trigger Classification

CLL treatment is generally started when active disease criteria are present, such as progressive marrow failure, massive or progressive/symptomatic splenomegaly, massive or progressive/symptomatic lymphadenopathy, progressive lymphocytosis, autoimmune cytopenias poorly responsive to corticosteroids, symptomatic extranodal disease, or disease-related symptoms such as weight loss, fatigue, fever, or night sweats.
