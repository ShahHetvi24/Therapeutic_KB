# Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) — Treatment Modalities


## 6. Treatment Modalities


### 6.1 Treatment Types

| Modality | Primary Role in CLL |
|---|---|
| Observation / watch-and-wait | Standard for many asymptomatic early-stage patients |
| BTK inhibitors | Continuous oral therapy or combination therapy; key frontline and relapsed class |
| BCL-2 inhibitor therapy | Venetoclax-based fixed-duration regimens, often with anti-CD20 antibody or BTKi |
| Anti-CD20 antibodies | Obinutuzumab or rituximab in combination regimens |
| Chemoimmunotherapy | Historically important; now limited to selected patients or regions |
| PI3K inhibitors | Older targeted class with toxicity and restricted use in many settings |
| Noncovalent BTK inhibitor | Pirtobrutinib after prior covalent BTKi exposure |
| CAR-T therapy | Late-line option after BTK inhibitor and BCL-2 inhibitor exposure |
| Supportive care | Infection prevention, immunoglobulin replacement in selected patients, vaccinations, transfusions, autoimmune cytopenia management |
| Clinical trials | Important across fixed-duration combinations, MRD-directed therapy, post-BTK/BCL-2 sequencing, and Richter transformation |

### 6.2 Treatment by Disease Area

#### 6.2.1 Newly Diagnosed, Asymptomatic CLL

Most patients with early-stage asymptomatic CLL are monitored without immediate treatment. This is commercially important because diagnosed prevalence is not equivalent to treated prevalence. [ESMO CLL Guideline Update 2024], [NCCN Patient Guidelines 2026]

#### 6.2.2 First-Line CLL Treatment

| Patient Segment | Common Treatment Strategy |
|---|---|
| Standard-risk, treatment-naive CLL | BTK inhibitor monotherapy, venetoclax + obinutuzumab, or acalabrutinib + venetoclax fixed-duration option |
| del(17p) / TP53-mutated CLL | Targeted therapy; chemoimmunotherapy generally avoided |
| Older or comorbid patients | Targeted therapy selected based on renal function, cardiac risk, bleeding risk, TLS risk, drug interactions, and patient preference |
| Patient preference for fixed-duration therapy | Venetoclax + obinutuzumab or all-oral acalabrutinib + venetoclax where label-appropriate |
| Patient preference for simpler continuous therapy | BTK inhibitor monotherapy, commonly acalabrutinib or zanubrutinib depending risk/tolerability/access |

In 2026, FDA approved **acalabrutinib plus venetoclax** for adults with CLL/SLL, creating the first US-approved all-oral, fixed-duration frontline combination regimen for CLL/SLL. [FDA Acalabrutinib + Venetoclax 2026], [AstraZeneca 2026 Release]

#### 6.2.3 Relapsed / Refractory CLL

| Prior Exposure | Potential Treatment Approach |
|---|---|
| Prior chemoimmunotherapy, no targeted therapy | BTK inhibitor or venetoclax-based targeted therapy |
| Prior covalent BTK inhibitor | Venetoclax-based therapy if not previously used; pirtobrutinib if appropriate |
| Prior venetoclax, no BTK inhibitor | BTK inhibitor therapy |
| Prior BTK inhibitor and BCL-2 inhibitor | Pirtobrutinib, liso-cel CAR-T in eligible patients, clinical trial, or alternative targeted therapy |
| Richter transformation | Treat as aggressive lymphoma; biopsy confirmation and separate treatment pathway required |

On December 3, 2025, FDA granted traditional approval to **pirtobrutinib** for adults with relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL previously treated with a covalent BTK inhibitor. [FDA Pirtobrutinib 2025]

On March 14, 2024, FDA approved **lisocabtagene maraleucel (Breyanzi)** for adult patients with relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL after at least two prior lines of therapy, including a BTK inhibitor and a BCL-2 inhibitor; this was the first CAR-T approval for CLL/SLL. [FDA Breyanzi], [CLL Society Liso-cel 2024]
