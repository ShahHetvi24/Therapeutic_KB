# Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) — Disease Overview


## 2. Disease Overview

### 2.1 Clinical Definition

CLL is a mature B-cell neoplasm characterized by clonal accumulation of small, mature-appearing but functionally abnormal B lymphocytes in peripheral blood, bone marrow, lymph nodes, and spleen. SLL is the tissue-predominant form of the same disease, with fewer circulating abnormal lymphocytes. [JNCCN CLL/SLL 2024], [ICD10Data C91.1]

### 2.2 Biology and Pathophysiology

CLL cells typically express B-cell antigens and a characteristic immunophenotype, commonly including CD5, CD19, CD20(dim), CD23, and light-chain restriction. Disease behavior varies widely, from indolent disease requiring no therapy for years to aggressive disease with rapid progression, cytopenias, bulky lymphadenopathy, autoimmune complications, or Richter transformation. [NCCN Patient Guidelines 2026], [JNCCN CLL/SLL Insights 2026]

### 2.3 Clinical Presentation

Many patients are diagnosed incidentally after routine blood work shows lymphocytosis. Symptomatic patients may present with lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, fatigue, recurrent infections, night sweats, fever, weight loss, anemia, thrombocytopenia, autoimmune hemolytic anemia, immune thrombocytopenia, or constitutional symptoms. [NCCN Patient Guidelines 2026], [ACS CLL Key Statistics 2026]

### 2.4 CLL-Specific Caveats

1. **Watch-and-wait is standard for asymptomatic early-stage disease.** Treatment should not be initiated solely because CLL is diagnosed; early treatment in asymptomatic disease has not shown overall survival benefit in major guideline discussions. [ESMO CLL Guideline Update 2024], [NCCN Patient Guidelines 2026]
2. **CLL and SLL are managed together but coded and captured differently across datasets.** CLL may appear under leukemia diagnosis codes, while SLL may appear under lymphoma codes or CLL/SLL combined categories. Claims algorithms should account for both depending on study objectives. [SEER CLL/SLL Stat Facts], [NCCN CLL/SLL Guidelines 2026]
3. **Treatment eligibility is not the same as prevalence.** Many prevalent patients are untreated or observed, so market sizing should distinguish diagnosed, active-treatment-eligible, treated, relapsed/refractory, and post-targeted-therapy populations. [NCCN Patient Guidelines 2026], [ACS CLL Key Statistics 2026]
4. **Line-of-therapy logic is complicated by continuous therapy.** BTK inhibitors may continue for years, while venetoclax-based regimens are often fixed-duration; duration alone should not be interpreted as disease progression. [FDA Acalabrutinib + Venetoclax 2026], [NCCN CLL/SLL Insights 2026]
5. **Richter transformation should be modelled separately.** Transformation to aggressive lymphoma has a poor prognosis and is treated differently from typical CLL progression. [JNCCN CLL/SLL Insights 2026], [NCCN CLL/SLL Guidelines 2026]
6. **Cardiac, bleeding, infection, and TLS risks drive treatment selection.** BTK inhibitors carry risks such as atrial fibrillation, bleeding, hypertension, infections, and cytopenias; venetoclax requires tumor lysis syndrome risk assessment and ramp-up monitoring. [FDA Acalabrutinib + Venetoclax 2026], [NCCN Patient Guidelines 2026]
7. **Resistance tracking matters.** BTK resistance may be associated with BTK or PLCG2 mutations, while venetoclax resistance may involve BCL2 mutations or clonal evolution; claims data alone rarely captures resistance biology. [NCCN CLL/SLL Insights 2026], [ESMO CLL Guideline Update 2024]
