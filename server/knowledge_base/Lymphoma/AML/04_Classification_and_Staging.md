# Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) — Classification and Staging


## 4. Classification and Staging

### 4.1 Classification Approach

AML does not use solid-tumor-style TNM staging. Instead, classification and risk stratification are based on disease biology, marrow/blood blast burden, cytogenetics, molecular mutations, antecedent hematologic disorders, therapy-related status, treatment fitness, and response/MRD. The 2025 AML update emphasizes that recent classification systems integrate new biological insights and that risk assessment is now heavily based on cytogenetic and molecular findings. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov]

### 4.2 Major AML Classification Buckets

| Classification Bucket | Description | Market / Treatment Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| De novo AML | AML arising without recognized preceding hematologic disease or prior cytotoxic therapy | Often treated by fitness, age, cytogenetics, and mutations |
| Secondary AML / AML after antecedent MDS or CMML | AML evolving from prior myeloid disease | Often adverse-risk; may influence use of CPX-351 or lower-intensity regimens |
| Therapy-related AML | AML following cytotoxic chemotherapy or radiation | Often high-risk; CPX-351 relevant in selected patients |
| AML with defining genetic abnormalities | Includes FLT3, IDH1/2, NPM1, KMT2A rearranged, core-binding factor AML, TP53, and others | Determines targeted therapy, transplant strategy, prognosis, and clinical trial eligibility |
| Acute promyelocytic leukemia | Distinct AML subtype driven by PML::RARA | Treated separately with ATRA/arsenic-based approaches; often excluded from general AML basket models |
| Relapsed/refractory AML | AML that returns after remission or fails to respond | High unmet need; mutation-directed salvage, menin inhibitors, transplant, and trials are key |

### 4.3 Functional “Staging” States for Analytics

| AML State | Operational Definition |
|---|---|
| Newly diagnosed AML | First AML diagnosis code or pathology-confirmed diagnosis with no prior AML therapy history |
| Induction phase | Initial treatment intended to induce remission |
| Remission / post-remission | Morphologic remission or claims proxy after induction/consolidation without active relapse codes |
| Consolidation | Post-remission therapy intended to eradicate residual disease |
| Maintenance | Continued lower-intensity therapy after remission, such as oral azacitidine in selected patients |
| Relapsed AML | AML relapse diagnosis code, restart of intensive/salvage therapy after remission, or re-emergence of AML-directed therapy after treatment gap |
| Refractory AML | Failure to achieve remission after induction or use of salvage therapy shortly after frontline treatment |
| Transplant episode | Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation and associated inpatient/outpatient care |
