# Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) — Disease Overview


## 2. Disease Overview

### 2.1 Clinical Definition

AML is an aggressive cancer of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells characterized by uncontrolled proliferation of immature myeloid blasts. Diagnosis is based on immature leukemia cells in peripheral blood, bone marrow, and/or extramedullary tissues, and modern AML classification integrates morphology, cytogenetics, molecular genetics, antecedent hematologic disorders, therapy-related history, and clinical behavior. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov]

### 2.2 Pathophysiology

AML develops when acquired genetic and epigenetic abnormalities disrupt normal myeloid differentiation and promote clonal expansion. Clinically, this results in bone marrow failure, anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, infection risk, bleeding risk, fatigue, and sometimes leukostasis or organ infiltration. The 2025 AML update notes that AML diagnosis, risk assessment, monitoring, and management have changed substantially because of improved biological understanding, molecular testing, MRD assessment, and approval of multiple new agents. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov]

### 2.3 Clinical Presentation

Common presenting features include fatigue, pallor, fever, infections, bruising, bleeding, dyspnea, bone pain, gum hypertrophy, skin lesions, or incidental abnormal blood counts. AML may present de novo, after antecedent myelodysplastic syndrome or chronic myelomonocytic leukemia, after prior chemotherapy/radiation as therapy-related AML, or as AML with defining molecular or cytogenetic abnormalities.

### 2.4 Severity and Progression

AML is generally considered a medical urgency because untreated disease can progress quickly and cause life-threatening cytopenias, infections, hemorrhage, leukostasis, disseminated intravascular coagulation in some subtypes, and organ dysfunction. AML remains a high-unmet-need blood cancer, with US 5-year relative survival reported at **33.4%** for 2016-2022 diagnoses. [seer.cancer.gov]
