# Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) — Treatment Modalities


## 6. Treatment Modalities


### 6.1 Treatment Types

#### 6.1.1 Core Treatment Modalities

| Modality | Role in AML |
|---|---|
| Intensive induction chemotherapy | Used for fit patients; typically cytarabine plus anthracycline backbone, with targeted additions depending on mutation status |
| Lower-intensity therapy | Used for older or unfit patients; hypomethylating agent plus venetoclax is a major standard approach |
| Targeted therapy | Used for FLT3, IDH1, IDH2, NPM1, KMT2A-rearranged, CD33-positive, and other biologically defined AML subsets |
| Consolidation chemotherapy | Used after remission to reduce relapse risk |
| Maintenance therapy | Used in selected post-remission patients, including oral azacitidine in appropriate settings |
| Allogeneic stem cell transplant | Potentially curative strategy for eligible patients, especially intermediate/adverse-risk AML or MRD-positive disease |
| Supportive care | Transfusions, antimicrobials, growth factor support in selected cases, TLS prophylaxis, antiemetics, infection management |
| Clinical trials | Especially important for TP53-mutated AML, relapsed/refractory AML, post-venetoclax relapse, and menin-inhibitor combinations |

#### 6.1.2 Treatment Selection Drivers

AML treatment selection depends on age, performance status, comorbidities, organ function, patient goals, cytogenetic/molecular risk, actionable mutations, history of prior myeloid disease, therapy-related status, WBC count, infection status, transplant eligibility, and MRD response. NCCN AML Guidelines Version 4.2026 and the 2025 AML update both emphasize molecular and risk-adapted management. [nccn.org], [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov]

### 6.2 Treatment by Disease Area

#### 6.2.1 Newly Diagnosed Intensive-Eligible AML

| Segment | Typical Treatment Approach |
|---|---|
| Fit, younger or medically eligible patients | Intensive induction chemotherapy, commonly cytarabine plus anthracycline backbone |
| FLT3-mutated AML | Midostaurin with induction/consolidation chemotherapy; gilteritinib is important in R/R FLT3-mutated disease |
| Core-binding factor AML | Intensive chemotherapy plus gemtuzumab ozogamicin may be used in CD33-positive disease |
| Therapy-related AML or AML-MRC / AML with myelodysplasia-related features | CPX-351 may be used in selected intensive-eligible patients |
| Post-remission | Consolidation chemotherapy and/or allogeneic transplant depending on risk and MRD |

Midostaurin was FDA-approved in 2017 for newly diagnosed FLT3 mutation-positive AML in combination with standard cytarabine and daunorubicin induction and cytarabine consolidation, based on a trial showing statistically significant overall survival improvement. [fda.gov]

#### 6.2.2 Newly Diagnosed Intensive-Ineligible AML

| Segment | Typical Treatment Approach |
|---|---|
| Age ≥75 or comorbidities precluding intensive induction | Venetoclax plus azacitidine, decitabine, or low-dose cytarabine |
| IDH1-mutated AML | Ivosidenib-based therapy, including combinations in eligible settings |
| IDH2-mutated AML | Enasidenib-based therapy in selected settings |
| Frail / poor tolerance | HMA monotherapy, low-dose cytarabine, hydroxyurea for cytoreduction, transfusion/supportive care, hospice/palliative approach where appropriate |

Venetoclax received accelerated approval for newly diagnosed AML in adults aged 75 years or older or with comorbidities precluding intensive induction, in combination with azacitidine, decitabine, or low-dose cytarabine. [fda.gov]

#### 6.2.3 Acute Promyelocytic Leukemia

APL is a distinct AML subtype and is usually modeled separately because treatment, prognosis, emergency management, and product baskets differ from non-APL AML. APL is covered within NCCN AML guideline materials but should be excluded or flagged separately in AML market analytics if the goal is non-APL AML treatment modeling. [nccn.org]

### 6.3 Relapsed Refractory Framework

#### 6.3.1 Definition

Relapsed AML refers to return of disease after achieving remission, while refractory AML refers to failure to achieve remission after initial therapy. In claims/EMR analytics, R/R AML may be inferred through relapse diagnosis codes, salvage therapy initiation, repeat induction, mutation-directed R/R therapy, transplant after salvage, or AML-directed therapy restart after a remission gap.

#### 6.3.2 Treatment Options

| R/R Segment | Common Treatment Options |
|---|---|
| FLT3-mutated R/R AML | Gilteritinib |
| IDH1-mutated R/R AML | Ivosidenib |
| IDH2-mutated R/R AML | Enasidenib |
| CD33-positive R/R AML | Gemtuzumab ozogamicin in selected patients |
| NPM1-mutated or KMT2A-rearranged AML | Menin inhibitors and clinical trials |
| Fit patients | Salvage chemotherapy followed by allogeneic transplant if response achieved |
| Older/unfit patients | Venetoclax-based combinations, targeted therapy, clinical trial, or supportive care |
| Post-venetoclax relapse | High unmet need; trials and mutation-directed strategies are important |

Revumenib and ziftomenib approvals in NPM1-mutated AML mark the emergence of menin inhibition as a major R/R AML category. AACR’s late-2025 oncology approval summary noted expanded revumenib approval for relapsed/refractory AML with susceptible NPM1 mutation and approval of ziftomenib for adult relapsed/refractory AML with susceptible NPM1 mutation. [aacr.org]
