# Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) — Epidemiology


## 3. Incidence, Prevalence and Statistics


### 3.1 Incidence

#### 3.1.1 Global Incidence

Global cancer burden estimates are maintained through the Global Cancer Observatory / GLOBOCAN platform, which provides incidence, mortality, and prevalence statistics across countries and cancer types. GLOBOCAN 2022 estimated nearly **20 million new cancer cases** and **9.7 million cancer deaths** worldwide, underscoring the broader global context in which hematologic malignancies are tracked. [gco.iarc.who.int], [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov]

For hematologic malignancies overall, a 2025 global burden analysis using GLOBOCAN 2022 and GBD 2021 reported that global hematologic malignancy case counts increased from 1990 to 2021/2022, while age-standardized death and DALY rates were generally stable or declining. The study also noted higher burden in older adults and males, and higher incidence in higher-SDI regions. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov]

**Important data note:** Public GLOBOCAN outputs often aggregate leukemias at a broader leukemia level, while AML-specific global incidence varies by source methodology, registry coverage, disease definitions, and coding schema. For AML-specific global modeling, use GBD leukemia subtype outputs, peer-reviewed AML burden studies, or claims/registry-derived market models rather than relying only on all-leukemia totals.

#### 3.1.2 United States Incidence

The American Cancer Society estimates that approximately **22,720 people** will be diagnosed with AML in the United States in 2026, including **12,160 males** and **10,560 females**. AML accounts for about **1 out of 3 leukemias in adults** and approximately **1% of all cancers**. [cancer.org]

SEER Cancer Stat Facts similarly reports **22,720 estimated new AML cases in 2026**, representing **1.1% of all new US cancer cases**. [seer.cancer.gov]

### 3.2 Prevalence

AML prevalence is lower than incidence might suggest because AML has relatively high early mortality compared with chronic hematologic malignancies such as CLL and because many patients require intensive, time-limited treatment rather than long-running chronic therapy. SEER reports AML 5-year relative survival of **33.4%**, which explains why the living patient pool is smaller than in diseases with high long-term survival. [seer.cancer.gov]

Prevalence is concentrated in older adults because AML incidence rises with age. ACS reports that AML is uncommon below age 45, most cases occur in adults, and the average age at diagnosis is approximately **69 years**. [cancer.org]

For market modeling, AML prevalence should be segmented into newly diagnosed active AML, post-remission surveillance, maintenance-treated patients, transplant survivors, relapsed/refractory AML, and patients receiving supportive or palliative care.
