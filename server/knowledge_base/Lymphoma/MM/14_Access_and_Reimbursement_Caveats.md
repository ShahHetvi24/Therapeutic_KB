# Multiple Myeloma (MM) — Access and Reimbursement Caveats


## 14. Access and Reimbursement Caveats

1. **Benefit design is split across medical and pharmacy claims.** Infused/injected biologics, bispecifics, CAR-T, and bone agents may appear on medical claims, while IMiDs, ixazomib, selinexor, venetoclax, and many supportive drugs appear on pharmacy claims. [Mordor Multiple Myeloma Market 2026-2031], [J&J Darzalex Billing]
2. **CAR-T reimbursement is episode-based and site-of-care sensitive.** Claims may include leukapheresis, bridging therapy, lymphodepletion, product acquisition, inpatient/outpatient infusion, CRS/ICANS treatment, and follow-up.
3. **Bispecific antibodies require REMS/step-up dose logic.** Teclistamab prescribing information includes boxed warnings for life-threatening or fatal CRS and neurologic toxicity/ICANS and is available through the Tecvayli-Talvey REMS. [FDA Teclistamab + Daratumumab 2026]
4. **Fixed versus continuous therapy changes revenue timing.** Maintenance and continuous relapse regimens create ongoing monthly spend, while CAR-T creates a high-cost episode and bispecifics create ongoing medical-benefit spend.
5. **Class exposure documentation drives authorization.** Many RRMM regimens require evidence of prior PI, IMiD, anti-CD38, and/or prior lines of therapy. [FDA Teclistamab + Daratumumab 2026]
6. **Generic erosion matters.** Lenalidomide, bortezomib, and other backbone drugs may remain clinically important while branded revenue declines.
7. **Smoldering MM creates a new access question.** High-risk SMM treatment with daratumumab/hyaluronidase expands the treatment boundary into precursor disease and may require careful payer-policy tracking. [AACR Oncology Approvals Q4 2025]
