# Multiple Myeloma (MM) — Diagnosis and Clinical Evaluation


## 5. Diagnosis and Clinical Evaluation

NCCN Version 5.2026 states that initial diagnostic workup should include history and physical examination, CBC with differential, peripheral smear, renal function, electrolytes, liver function tests, calcium, uric acid, albumin, LDH, beta-2 microglobulin, and other testing to distinguish symptomatic from asymptomatic MM. It also notes the need to distinguish MM from other plasma-cell neoplasms/dyscrasias. [JNCCN MM Version 5.2026]

| Workup Component | Role |
|---|---|
| CBC with differential | Detects anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia |
| Serum chemistry | Calcium, creatinine, albumin, renal function, electrolytes |
| Serum protein electrophoresis / immunofixation | Detects and characterizes M protein |
| Urine protein electrophoresis / immunofixation | Detects urinary monoclonal protein and light chains |
| Serum free light chains | Assesses light-chain disease and risk |
| Quantitative immunoglobulins | Measures immunoparesis and monoclonal protein burden |
| Bone marrow biopsy / aspirate | Confirms clonal plasma cells and allows cytogenetics/FISH |
| FISH / cytogenetics | Detects high-risk abnormalities such as del(17p), t(4;14), t(14;16), and 1q gain/amplification |
| Imaging | Whole-body low-dose CT, PET/CT, MRI depending presentation and guideline/practice |
| LDH and beta-2 microglobulin | Tumor burden and prognosis |
| MRD testing | Depth of response in trials and selected clinical settings |
| Renal and bone assessment | Evaluates CRAB features and supportive-care needs |
| Cardiac biomarkers | NT-proBNP / BNP where amyloidosis or cardiac involvement is a concern; NCCN notes NT-proBNP in workup context. [JNCCN MM Version 5.2026] |
