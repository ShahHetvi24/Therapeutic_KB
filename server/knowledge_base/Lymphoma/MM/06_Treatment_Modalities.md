# Multiple Myeloma (MM) — Treatment Modalities


## 6. Treatment Modalities


### 6.1 Treatment Types

| Modality | Role in MM |
|---|---|
| Proteasome inhibitors | Bortezomib, carfilzomib, ixazomib; core class across frontline and relapse |
| IMiDs / CELMoDs | Lenalidomide, pomalidomide, thalidomide; backbone of induction, maintenance, and relapse therapy |
| Anti-CD38 antibodies | Daratumumab and isatuximab; central in frontline quadruplets and relapse combinations |
| Corticosteroids | Dexamethasone/prednisone; common combination partners |
| Alkylator chemotherapy | Melphalan, cyclophosphamide; transplant conditioning and selected regimens |
| Autologous stem cell transplant | Standard consolidation option for eligible patients |
| Maintenance therapy | Lenalidomide-based or risk-adapted maintenance after induction/transplant |
| BCMA CAR-T | Ide-cel and cilta-cel in relapsed/refractory settings depending label |
| Bispecific antibodies | BCMAxCD3, GPRC5DxCD3, and emerging targets; expanding into earlier relapse |
| Bone-modifying agents | Zoledronic acid, denosumab; skeletal-related event prevention |
| Radiation therapy | Palliation or local control for plasmacytoma/bone pain/spinal cord compression |
| Supportive care | Antiviral prophylaxis, VTE prophylaxis, IVIG in selected patients, renal support, antimicrobial management, pain control |

### 6.2 Treatment by Disease Area

#### 6.2.1 Newly Diagnosed Transplant-Eligible MM

| Treatment Phase | Common Strategy |
|---|---|
| Induction | Anti-CD38 + PI + IMiD + dexamethasone quadruplet increasingly common; VRd or Dara-VRd-like approaches depending label/access |
| Stem cell collection | Collection after induction; avoid prolonged lenalidomide before collection when possible depending practice |
| Autologous transplant | High-dose melphalan with autologous stem cell rescue |
| Consolidation | Additional cycles in selected patients depending response/risk |
| Maintenance | Lenalidomide-based maintenance; intensified maintenance in high-risk patients depending guideline/practice |

In early 2026, FDA-approved use of daratumumab/hyaluronidase with bortezomib, lenalidomide, and dexamethasone expanded anti-CD38 quadruplet therapy for newly diagnosed MM, including transplant-ineligible contexts, and is part of broader frontline intensification. [AACR FDA Approvals Jan-Mar 2026], [J&J Darzalex Billing]

#### 6.2.2 Newly Diagnosed Transplant-Ineligible MM

| Patient Segment | Common Strategy |
|---|---|
| Fit older adult | Anti-CD38 + lenalidomide + dexamethasone or anti-CD38/PI/IMiD-based regimen depending label and tolerance |
| Frail older adult | Dose-adjusted anti-CD38, lenalidomide, bortezomib, and dexamethasone-based regimens |
| Renal impairment | Bortezomib-based therapy often important due to rapid disease control and renal recovery potential |
| High-risk cytogenetics | PI-containing and anti-CD38-containing regimens often favored; maintenance intensification may be considered |

#### 6.2.3 Smoldering Multiple Myeloma

Most smoldering MM has historically been managed with observation until progression to active MM. However, in late 2025, daratumumab and hyaluronidase was approved as monotherapy for adults with high-risk smoldering multiple myeloma, marking the first approved intervention for this precursor-state population and creating a new market segment. [AACR Oncology Approvals Q4 2025]

#### 6.2.4 Relapsed / Refractory MM

| Prior Exposure / Refractoriness | Common Options |
|---|---|
| Lenalidomide-refractory after 1L | Daratumumab-based, carfilzomib-based, pomalidomide-based, or bispecific-based regimens depending prior exposure |
| Anti-CD38-naive relapse | Daratumumab or isatuximab-based combinations |
| PI-exposed / PI-sensitive | Carfilzomib, bortezomib, or ixazomib combinations |
| Triple-class exposed | BCMA CAR-T, BCMA bispecific, GPRC5D bispecific, selinexor, clinical trials |
| BCMA-naive late-line | BCMA CAR-T or BCMA bispecific may be considered depending eligibility/access |
| BCMA-exposed | Non-BCMA target therapy such as GPRC5D bispecific, FcRH5 strategies, clinical trials |
| Frail / infection-prone | Toxicity-adjusted regimens and supportive care are critical |

On March 5, 2026, FDA approved teclistamab plus daratumumab/hyaluronidase for adult RRMM patients who had received at least one prior line including a proteasome inhibitor and an immunomodulatory agent. The same action converted teclistamab monotherapy from accelerated to traditional approval for adults with RRMM after at least four prior lines including a PI, IMiD, and anti-CD38 antibody. [FDA Teclistamab + Daratumumab 2026]

### 6.3 Relapsed Refractory Framework

#### 6.3.1 Definitions

Relapsed MM means disease returns after response or remission. Refractory MM generally means disease progresses on therapy or within a short time after therapy. MM treatment analytics should distinguish relapsed, refractory, double-refractory, triple-class exposed, triple-class refractory, penta-exposed, and BCMA-exposed patients.

#### 6.3.2 Class Exposure Definitions

| Term | Operational Meaning |
|---|---|
| PI-exposed | Prior bortezomib, carfilzomib, or ixazomib |
| PI-refractory | Progression on or shortly after PI therapy |
| IMiD-exposed | Prior lenalidomide, pomalidomide, or thalidomide |
| Lenalidomide-refractory | Progression on or shortly after lenalidomide; key early-relapse segmentation variable |
| Anti-CD38-exposed | Prior daratumumab or isatuximab |
| Triple-class exposed | Prior PI, IMiD, and anti-CD38 antibody |
| Triple-class refractory | Refractory to at least one PI, one IMiD, and one anti-CD38 antibody |
| BCMA-exposed | Prior BCMA CAR-T, BCMA bispecific, or BCMA ADC |
| Penta-refractory | Refractory to two PIs, two IMiDs, and one anti-CD38 antibody |

#### 6.3.3 Key R/R Market Segments

| Segment | Strategic Importance |
|---|---|
| First relapse, lenalidomide-refractory | Very large and commercially important as frontline lenalidomide maintenance is common |
| Early RRMM after PI + IMiD | Tecvayli + Darzalex Faspro approval creates an earlier-line immunotherapy option. [FDA Teclistamab + Daratumumab 2026] |
| Triple-class exposed | Major eligibility segment for CAR-T and bispecifics |
| BCMA-naive late-line | Valuable pool for BCMA CAR-T and BCMA bispecifics |
| BCMA-exposed relapse | Drives need for GPRC5D, FcRH5, CELMoDs, and non-BCMA strategies |
| Frail / infection-prone relapse | Requires dose adjustment and supportive-care market capture |
