# Multiple Myeloma (MM) — Disease Overview


## 2. Disease Overview

### 2.1 Clinical Definition

Multiple myeloma is a malignant neoplasm of plasma cells that typically accumulates in bone marrow and produces monoclonal immunoglobulin or light-chain protein. NCCN describes MM as a plasma-cell malignancy that can lead to bone destruction, elevated calcium, anemia, and kidney damage from secreted monoclonal protein. [JNCCN MM Version 5.2026]

### 2.2 Pathophysiology

MM arises from clonal plasma cells that proliferate in the bone marrow microenvironment, produce monoclonal protein, suppress normal hematopoiesis, activate osteoclasts, inhibit osteoblasts, impair immunity, and damage kidneys through light-chain and monoclonal protein effects. Clinical features are commonly remembered using **CRAB** criteria: hyperCalcemia, Renal dysfunction, Anemia, and Bone lesions. [JNCCN MM Version 5.2026], [NCCN Patient Guidelines MM 2026]

### 2.3 Disease Spectrum

| Disease State | Description | Market Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| MGUS | Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance; precursor plasma-cell disorder | Usually monitored; not treated as active MM |
| Smoldering multiple myeloma (SMM) | Asymptomatic plasma-cell disorder with higher tumor burden than MGUS but no myeloma-defining end-organ damage | Traditionally observed; high-risk SMM now has approved intervention with daratumumab/hyaluronidase in late 2025. [AACR Oncology Approvals Q4 2025] |
| Active symptomatic MM | Plasma-cell malignancy with myeloma-defining events or CRAB features | Main treated MM market |
| Relapsed MM | Disease returns after prior response | Drives repeated LOTs and large recurring market |
| Refractory MM | Disease progresses on or shortly after therapy | Key eligibility concept for later-line therapies |
| Triple-class exposed | Prior exposure to PI, IMiD, and anti-CD38 antibody | Important RRMM treatment segment |
| Penta-exposed / penta-refractory | Exposure or refractoriness to multiple PI, IMiD, and anti-CD38 drugs | High-unmet-need late-line population |
| Plasma cell leukemia | Leukemic plasma-cell neoplasm | Distinct high-risk plasma-cell disorder; should be separated analytically |
| Solitary plasmacytoma | Localized plasma-cell tumor | Usually local therapy and surveillance; not the same as systemic MM |

### 2.4 MM-Specific Caveats

1. **MM is usually treated as a chronic relapsing disease rather than a single-event cancer.** Patients often receive multiple sequential therapy lines over many years, so market sizing should include longitudinal treatment exposure and cumulative class refractoriness. [NCCN MM Guidelines 2026]
2. **Transplant eligibility strongly affects frontline pathways.** Newly diagnosed patients are typically segmented as transplant-eligible or transplant-ineligible, with different induction, consolidation, and maintenance patterns. [JNCCN MM Version 5.2026]
3. **Maintenance therapy can last years.** Lenalidomide or other maintenance may continue long after induction and transplant; maintenance should be separated from progression-driven new LOT. [NCCN MM Guidelines 2026]
4. **High-risk cytogenetics change prognosis and treatment intensity.** del(17p), t(4;14), t(14;16), t(14;20), gain/amp 1q, and complex biology should be captured when available. [JNCCN MM Version 5.2026]
5. **CRAB features and myeloma-defining events are essential for active disease classification.** MGUS and smoldering MM should not be automatically counted as active treated MM unless treatment criteria are met. [NCCN Patient Guidelines MM 2026]
6. **BCMA exposure matters.** Later-line sequencing now requires distinguishing BCMA-naive versus BCMA-exposed patients because CAR-T, bispecifics, and ADCs may target the same antigen. [FDA Teclistamab + Daratumumab 2026]
7. **REMS and step-up dosing affect real-world uptake.** Bispecific antibodies such as teclistamab, talquetamab, and elranatamab require CRS/ICANS monitoring and REMS or restricted-program processes, which affect site of care and claims interpretation. [FDA Teclistamab + Daratumumab 2026], [CareCostEstimate Elrexfio J1323]
8. **Supportive care is a major part of MM cost and outcomes.** Bone-modifying agents, antivirals, antibacterial/IVIG support, VTE prophylaxis, renal care, pain management, and infection prevention should be included in total cost models. [NCCN Patient Guidelines MM 2026]
