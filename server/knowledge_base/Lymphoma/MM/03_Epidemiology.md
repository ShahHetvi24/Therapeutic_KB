# Multiple Myeloma (MM) — Epidemiology


## 3. Incidence, Prevalence and Statistics


### 3.1 Incidence

#### 3.1.1 Global Incidence

A JNCI analysis using GLOBOCAN 2022 estimated approximately **188,000 global MM cases** and **121,000 deaths** in 2022. Eastern Asia accounted for about **21%** of cases and Northern America for **19%**, followed by South-Central Asia at **11%** and Western Europe at **9%**. The same study projected that MM incidence and mortality could increase by **71%** and **79%**, respectively, by 2045 if rates remain unchanged, largely reflecting aging and population growth. [JNCI Global MM Burden 2025]

#### 3.1.2 United States Incidence

The American Cancer Society estimates approximately **36,000 new MM cases** in the United States in 2026, including **20,150 in men** and **15,850 in women**. ACS also estimates **10,850 deaths** in 2026, including **5,780 in men** and **5,070 in women**. [ACS MM Key Statistics 2026]

SEER Cancer Stat Facts reports **36,000 estimated new myeloma cases in 2026**, representing **1.7% of all new US cancer cases**, and **10,850 estimated deaths**, representing **1.7% of all US cancer deaths**. SEER reports **5-year relative survival of 63.7%** for 2016-2022 diagnoses. [SEER Myeloma Stat Facts]

#### 3.1.3 Demographics

MM is primarily a disease of older adults. ACS reports that most people diagnosed with MM are at least **65 years** old and that the average age at diagnosis is **69**. Less than **1%** of MM cases are diagnosed in people younger than 35. Lifetime risk in the United States is less than 1%, approximately **1 in 108 for men** and **1 in 133 for women**. [ACS MM Key Statistics 2026]

### 3.2 Prevalence

MM prevalence has increased as survival improves and patients receive multiple sequential lines of therapy. SEER reports 5-year relative survival of **63.7%** for 2016-2022 diagnoses, while SEER trend data show survival gains over time. [SEER Myeloma Stat Facts]

For market analytics, prevalence should be segmented into newly diagnosed transplant-eligible MM, newly diagnosed transplant-ineligible MM, smoldering MM, maintenance-treated patients, first relapse, lenalidomide-refractory patients, anti-CD38-exposed patients, triple-class exposed patients, BCMA-naive late-line patients, BCMA-exposed late-line patients, CAR-T-treated patients, bispecific-treated patients, high-risk cytogenetic patients, and supportive-care-only patients. [NCCN MM Guidelines 2026], [JNCCN MM Version 5.2026]
