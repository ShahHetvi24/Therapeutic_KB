# Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) — Treatment Modalities


## 6. Treatment Modalities


### 6.1 Treatment Types

| Modality | Role in NHL |
|---|---|
| Watch-and-wait | Used in selected asymptomatic indolent lymphomas |
| Chemoimmunotherapy | R-CHOP, R-CHP, BR, R-CVP, R-EPOCH, and other regimens depending subtype |
| Anti-CD20 antibodies | Rituximab and obinutuzumab-based backbones in B-cell NHL |
| ADCs | Polatuzumab vedotin, brentuximab vedotin, loncastuximab tesirine |
| CAR-T therapy | CD19-directed cellular therapy for R/R LBCL, FL, MCL, and other approved settings |
| Bispecific antibodies | CD20xCD3 agents such as epcoritamab, glofitamab, mosunetuzumab |
| BTK inhibitors | MCL, MZL, Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia, and selected B-cell NHL contexts |
| EZH2 inhibitor | Tazemetostat in selected R/R follicular lymphoma |
| Radiation therapy | Localized indolent lymphoma, bulky disease consolidation, palliation, CNS or extranodal management in selected cases |
| Stem cell transplant | Autologous transplant in selected chemosensitive relapse; allogeneic transplant in select high-risk settings |
| Supportive care | Infection prophylaxis, growth factors, transfusions, TLS prophylaxis, CRS/ICANS management for immune therapies |

### 6.2 Treatment by Disease Area

#### 6.2.1 Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)

| Setting | Common Treatment Approach |
|---|---|
| Frontline standard risk | R-CHOP or Polivy-R-CHP depending patient risk, guideline preference, access, and local practice |
| High-grade / double-hit biology | More intensive regimens or clinical trial depending cytogenetics and patient fitness |
| Primary refractory or early relapse | CAR-T therapy in eligible patients; bridging therapy may be used |
| Late relapse, transplant eligible | Salvage chemoimmunotherapy followed by autologous transplant in chemosensitive disease |
| Transplant/CAR-T ineligible or later lines | Bispecific antibodies, ADCs, tafasitamab-lenalidomide, loncastuximab, polatuzumab-based therapy, clinical trial |

#### 6.2.2 Follicular Lymphoma (FL)

| Setting | Common Treatment Approach |
|---|---|
| Low tumor burden / asymptomatic | Watch-and-wait or rituximab monotherapy in selected patients |
| Symptomatic frontline | Anti-CD20 plus chemotherapy, bendamustine-rituximab, obinutuzumab-based therapy, or lenalidomide-rituximab depending patient factors |
| Relapsed after one or more lines | Lenalidomide-rituximab, epcoritamab + R2, anti-CD20-based therapy, PI3K alternatives in limited contexts, clinical trial |
| After two or more lines | Epcoritamab monotherapy, mosunetuzumab, CAR-T, tazemetostat for EZH2-mutated or selected patients, other systemic options |
| Suspected transformation | Biopsy; treat as aggressive lymphoma if confirmed |

On November 18, 2025, FDA approved epcoritamab with lenalidomide and rituximab for relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma and granted traditional approval to epcoritamab monotherapy after two or more systemic lines. In EPCORE FL-1, epcoritamab plus R2 showed PFS superiority with HR 0.21 and ORR 89% versus 74% with R2 alone. [FDA Epcoritamab FL 2025]

#### 6.2.3 Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL)

| Setting | Common Treatment Approach |
|---|---|
| Fit frontline | Intensive chemoimmunotherapy ± autologous transplant and maintenance rituximab in selected patients |
| Less fit frontline | Bendamustine-rituximab, BTK inhibitor-containing strategies, or other lower-intensity regimens according to guideline and access |
| Relapsed/refractory | BTK inhibitors, CAR-T therapy, noncovalent BTK inhibitor strategies, clinical trials |
| High-risk TP53 / blastoid | Clinical trial strongly favored where feasible |

#### 6.2.4 Marginal Zone Lymphoma (MZL)

| Setting | Common Treatment Approach |
|---|---|
| Localized extranodal disease | Local therapy, radiation, infection-directed therapy where appropriate |
| Systemic treatment needed | Rituximab-based therapy, bendamustine-rituximab, BTK inhibitor in relapsed settings |
| Relapsed/refractory | BTK inhibitor, anti-CD20 combinations, clinical trial |

#### 6.2.5 Peripheral T-Cell Lymphoma (PTCL) and Other T-Cell NHL

| Setting | Common Treatment Approach |
|---|---|
| CD30-positive PTCL | Brentuximab vedotin + CHP in appropriate patients |
| Other PTCL frontline | CHOP-like therapy, clinical trial, transplant consolidation in selected responders |
| Relapsed/refractory | Brentuximab for CD30-positive disease, pralatrexate, HDAC inhibitors where available, clinical trial, transplant in selected patients |
| Cutaneous T-cell lymphoma | Skin-directed therapy, systemic agents, mogamulizumab, brentuximab for CD30-positive disease, extracorporeal photopheresis in selected cases |

### 6.3 Relapsed Refractory Framework

#### 6.3.1 R/R NHL Definition

Relapsed NHL means disease returns after response or remission. Refractory NHL means disease fails to respond adequately to treatment or progresses during therapy or shortly after therapy. In analytics, R/R status can be inferred through relapse/progression diagnosis codes, new systemic therapy after prior NHL treatment, CAR-T referral, bispecific initiation, salvage chemotherapy, transplant, or shift to later-line product baskets.

#### 6.3.2 R/R Treatment Overview

| R/R Segment | Major Treatment Options |
|---|---|
| R/R DLBCL, CAR-T eligible | Yescarta, Breyanzi, Kymriah depending label, line, and patient eligibility |
| R/R DLBCL, CAR-T ineligible or post-CAR-T | Epcoritamab, glofitamab, loncastuximab, polatuzumab-based therapy, tafasitamab-lenalidomide, clinical trial |
| R/R FL after ≥2 lines | Mosunetuzumab, epcoritamab, CAR-T, tazemetostat, lenalidomide-rituximab, clinical trial |
| R/R MCL | BTK inhibitors, Tecartus, Breyanzi where label-appropriate, pirtobrutinib in selected settings, clinical trial |
| R/R MZL | BTK inhibitors, anti-CD20 therapy, clinical trial |
| R/R PTCL | Brentuximab if CD30-positive, pralatrexate, HDAC inhibitors where available, clinical trial |
| Post-CAR-T relapse | Bispecific antibody, ADC, clinical trial, allo-transplant in selected patients |
