# Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) — Access and Reimbursement Caveats


## 14. Access and Reimbursement Caveats

1. **CAR-T requires multi-step billing.** Claims may include leukapheresis, bridging therapy, lymphodepletion, product acquisition, inpatient or outpatient infusion, CRS/ICANS management, and follow-up monitoring. [ASTCT CAR-T Coding Guide 2026]
2. **Bispecific antibodies require step-up dosing and toxicity monitoring.** CRS and ICANS boxed warnings affect site-of-care, payer authorization, and early-cycle observation requirements. [FDA Epcoritamab FL 2025], [Epkinly Billing Codes]
3. **Anti-CD20 therapies may be billed under multiple product and biosimilar codes.** Rituximab analytics must group biosimilars carefully to avoid undercounting. [CMS Rituximab Billing Article]
4. **Oral agents may be undercounted in medical-only data.** Lenalidomide, BTK inhibitors, tazemetostat, and other oral agents are often captured on pharmacy claims.
5. **Subtype mismatch can distort market share.** A product may dominate a narrow segment, such as CAR-T product share or frontline DLBCL ADC share, without leading the total NHL market.
6. **Payer policies often require prior therapy documentation.** Bispecific and CAR-T approvals frequently require prior anti-CD20 therapy, anthracycline exposure, number of prior lines, or CAR-T/transplant eligibility documentation.
7. **Biosimilar substitution affects branded market share.** Rituximab remains a clinical backbone, but revenue share for branded Rituxan may be lower due to biosimilar uptake.
