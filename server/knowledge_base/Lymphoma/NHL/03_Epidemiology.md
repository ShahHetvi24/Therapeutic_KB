# Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma (NHL) — Epidemiology


## 3. Incidence, Prevalence and Statistics


### 3.1 Incidence

#### 3.1 Global Incidence

NHL is one of the major hematologic malignancy categories globally. A 2025 global burden analysis using GLOBOCAN 2022 and GBD 2021 reported that hematologic malignancy case counts increased from 1990 to 2021/2022, with NHL specifically contributing to the rising global burden. Incidence varies by geography, age structure, HIV prevalence, infection exposure, diagnostic capacity, registry quality, and socioeconomic development. [Global Burden Hematologic Malignancies 2025]

#### 3.2 United States Incidence

The American Cancer Society estimates that **79,320 people** in the United States will be diagnosed with NHL in 2026, including **43,770 males** and **35,550 females**. ACS also estimates **19,970 deaths** from NHL in 2026, including **11,710 males** and **8,260 females**. [ACS NHL Key Statistics 2026]

SEER reports the same 2026 estimate of **79,320 new NHL cases**, representing **3.8% of all new US cancer cases**, and **19,970 deaths**, representing **3.2% of all US cancer deaths**. SEER reports **5-year relative survival of 74.3%** for diagnoses during 2016-2022. [SEER NHL Stat Facts]

---

### 3.2 Prevalence

NHL prevalence is high because many patients survive years after diagnosis, especially in indolent subtypes, and because aggressive NHL cure rates have improved with immunochemotherapy, CAR-T, and newer immune therapies. SEER and ACS survival statistics show substantial survival improvement versus historical eras, with current 5-year relative survival at approximately **74.3%**. [SEER NHL Stat Facts], [ACS NHL Key Statistics 2026]

For market analytics, prevalence should be segmented into: newly diagnosed active NHL, watch-and-wait indolent NHL, first-line treated aggressive NHL, post-frontline remission/surveillance, relapsed/refractory aggressive NHL, relapsed indolent NHL, transformed lymphoma, CAR-T-eligible patients, bispecific-eligible patients, post-CAR-T relapse, and T-cell NHL high-unmet-need cohorts. [NCCN B-Cell Lymphomas Guidelines 2026]
